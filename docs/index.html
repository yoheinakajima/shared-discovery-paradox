A group can get better information and, at the same time, find its target less frequently. Collected clues make the ranking of the boxes better. But a one-answer rule then sends all attempts to the top of that ranking. A portfolio of 8 searches becomes 1 search, done 8 times.

A group can become better informed and less likely to discover what it is looking for. Sharing evidence genuinely improves the ranking of candidates — and a one-answer rule then spends every attempt on the top of that ranking, collapsing a portfolio of eight searches into one search, repeated eight times.

The difference between Q and G is redundancy. The identity is N·Q − G = E[(K−1)⁺] — the expected number of successful actions that hit the same box again. Consensus gets its high action quality because all 8 actions are the same action. On average, 2.68 of its hits open a box that is already open.

The gap between the columns is not mysterious — it is redundancy, exactly: N·Q − G = E[(K−1)⁺], the expected number of redundant successful actions. Consensus maximizes per-action quality by making all eight actions the same action: 2.68 of its expected hits land on a box that was already open.

Partially. Remove the planner. The collected reports stay public. 8 searchers divide a prize of 1 between the searchers that open the jackpot box. A crowd makes each part of the prize smaller. Partially. Remove the planner, keep the pooled reports public, and let eight self-interested searchers split a unit prize among whoever opens the jackpot. The opposite case shows the domain of the paradox. Keep the clue accuracy constant while the world becomes larger. The evidence becomes abundant, and consensus becomes correct — it goes to 1. The paradox is a property of searches with sparse evidence: new science, pre-seed investment, new hypotheses. In these areas, organizations use consensus the most.

The flip side sharpens the domain. Hold clue accuracy fixed while the world grows — evidence becomes abundant — and consensus becomes consistent, rising toward one. Share the evidence.
